How It Works

Smart Suction, Simplified

The Old Way

JP bulbs require constant manual squeezing, safety-pinning to clothing, and messy emptying. Unchanged since 1974.

The SOMAVAC® Way

As flat as a phone. Worn discreetly under clothing. Continuous smart suction. No squeezing, no pins, no mess.

01

Connect

The surgeon connects SOMAVAC® to up to 4 surgical drains via one-way connectors, creating a clean, fully closed system that minimizes exposure to infectious materials.1

02

Wear & Go

As flat as a phone, the pump attaches to a belt worn discreetly under clothing. Fluid collects in a marked bag for easy measurement and disposal.1,2 No safety pins. No “grenade” bulbs.

03

Recover Faster

Continuous sensor-controlled suction maintains −100mmHg, delivering Deep Tissue Negative Pressure Therapy directly into the wound space, removing waste fluid continuously. Tissue layers heal together. Drains out up to 30% sooner.*1,3

Bring SOMAVAC to Your Practice
The SOMAVAC Answer

The First & Only Deep Tissue Negative Pressure Therapy

Negative pressure wound therapy is proven and standard of care, but until now it only worked at the surface. SOMAVAC® uses continuous sensor-controlled suction to deliver −100mmHg negative pressure directly into the deep wound space1,4, where it matters most.

This deep tissue therapy removes stagnant fluid, creates tissue apposition so layers heal together properly, and enables integration of acellular dermal matrix (ADM) tissue. The result: drains out up to 30% sooner*, fewer seromas, and a reduced risk of complications.1

Worn discreetly on a belt under clothing and as flat as a phone, the fully closed system accepts up to 4 surgical drains through one-way connectors, eliminating manual bulb compression and minimizing infection risk.1

The SOMAVAC® Sustained Vacuum System (SVS) is intended for the removal of bodily fluids following large flap forming plastic and general surgeries. There are potential risks associated with the use of a SOMAVAC SVS including a temporary lack of suction which could result in minor pain or engorgement because of some fluid retention. Talk to your surgeon about indications, warnings, precautions, adverse events and other important medical information. This information is for educational purposes only.
